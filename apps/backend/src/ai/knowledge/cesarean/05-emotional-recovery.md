# Emotional Recovery After a C-Section

Recovering from surgery **and** caring for a newborn is demanding. Mood ups and downs ("baby blues") are common in the first 1–2 weeks.

## Normal
- Feeling tired, tearful, or overwhelmed in the first couple of weeks, gradually improving.
- Some disappointment or processing if the cesarean was unplanned — this is common.

## When to seek help (postpartum depression / anxiety)
Contact a clinician if, beyond ~2 weeks, you have:
- Persistent **low mood, hopelessness, or loss of interest**.
- **Severe mood changes**, intense anxiety, or trouble bonding with the baby.
- **Any thoughts of harming yourself or your baby** → seek help urgently.

## What helps
- Rest when you can, accept practical help, and stay connected to people.
- Gentle daily walks (also good for physical recovery and clot prevention).
- Talk to your midwife, GP, or health visitor early — postpartum depression is common and treatable.

> The AI Recovery Nurse should respond with empathy, normalize common feelings, and **escalate to the doctor** on any self-harm signal or persistent severe low mood.

# C-Section Recovery: Overview & Timeline

A cesarean section is major abdominal surgery; full recovery typically takes **about 6 weeks**, though it varies.

## In hospital (day 0–2)
- Most people stay in hospital **1–2 days** after the operation.
- A urinary **catheter** stays in place for **at least 12 hours**.
- The wound dressing is kept on for **at least 24 hours**.
- **Early mobilisation is encouraged** — getting up and moving soon after surgery speeds recovery and lowers blood-clot risk.
- You can usually eat and drink as soon as you feel hungry.

## First weeks at home
- **Pain/tenderness around the wound** is common for **several weeks**; sharp pain usually eases after the first **2–3 days**.
- **Vaginal bleeding (lochia)** is normal for **up to 6 weeks**, gradually changing from red to brown to white/yellow. Use **maternity/period pads, not tampons** (tampons raise infection risk).
- Non-dissolvable **stitches or staples are removed around day 5–7** by a nurse (dissolvable stitches do not need removal).
- Rest when you can, accept help, and keep gentle daily walks going.

## Around 6 weeks
- Most people can resume normal activities (driving, exercise, sex, heavier lifting) **once they feel comfortable, usually by ~6 weeks** — see the activity guidance.
- A **postnatal check** is typically done around 6 weeks.

## Recovery is not linear
Good days and bad days are expected. Worsening pain, new fever, or heavy bleeding are **not** part of normal recovery — see the warning-signs guidance.
